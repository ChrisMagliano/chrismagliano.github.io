Learn the concepts

Why chronological backtesting?

Random train/test splitting leaks future information into time-series evaluation. Expanding-window backtesting fits models on the past and evaluates genuinely future periods.

What do MAE, RMSE, and sMAPE measure?

MAE is the average absolute forecast error. RMSE gives greater weight to large errors. sMAPE scales errors by the magnitude of actual and predicted values; it can behave poorly near zero.

Why is the seasonal model unavailable?

Seasonal Naive requires at least two full seasonal cycles in every training fold. The candidate seasonal periods are 7 for daily, 12 for monthly, and 52 for weekly data.

How reliable are prediction intervals?

Intervals use historical out-of-sample forecast errors. They are displayed only when at least 20 non-overlapping backtest folds provide an error for each horizon. These empirical intervals are not guaranteed to achieve 90% future coverage.

Is the best model guaranteed to win in the future?

No. Backtest results are estimates based on historical data. Selecting the winner using these same results can make the winner's reported performance optimistic. Forecasts assume future behavior remains sufficiently similar to the past.
